Property Valuation in Singapore: How It Caps Your Loan, and What to Do When It Comes In Low

By Dan Ler, Mortgage Advisor

Short answer

Property valuation decides how much you can borrow. In Singapore your loan is a percentage of the lower of the purchase price or the valuation, not of the price you agreed. Your CPF usage is capped the same way. So if a flat is bought for S$550,000 and valued at S$500,000, a 75% loan is S$375,000, not S$412,500, and on an HDB resale flat the S$50,000 above valuation must be paid in cash. Get an indicative valuation before you commit to a price, from more than one bank if it is close.

Why Valuation, Not Price, Sets Your Loan

A lender's protection is the property, so it lends against what the property is worth to an independent valuer, not against what you were willing to pay. Under the MAS loan-to-value rules, the limit, 75% on a first housing loan, is applied to the lower of the purchase price or the valuation. HDB does the same for its concessionary loan.

CPF follows the same logic. The amount of Ordinary Account savings you can use is tied to the Valuation Limit, the lower of the price or the value, with a further allowance above it in some cases. Our CPF OA housing guide covers the Valuation Limit and the Withdrawal Limit in full.

The result is simple: every dollar you pay above valuation is a dollar that neither the bank nor your CPF will cover.

A Worked Example

The loan shrinks by three-quarters of the gap, and you fund the gap itself.

Valued at the priceValued S$50,000 below
Agreed priceS$550,000S$550,000
ValuationS$550,000S$500,000
Loan at 75% of the lower figureS$412,500S$375,000
Downpayment at 25% of the lower figureS$137,500S$125,000
Amount above valuationnilS$50,000
Total you fund before stamp dutyS$137,500S$175,000

A S$50,000 gap between price and valuation costs you S$37,500 more up front, because the loan shrinks by three-quarters of the gap and you fund the gap itself. On an HDB resale flat that S$50,000 is cash over valuation and cannot come from CPF, which is often the harder part to find.

“A valuation shortfall does not change the price. It changes who pays it: the bank stops at the valuation, and you pick up everything above it.”

HDB Resale: the Request for Value

You see HDB's value before you exercise the OTP, so a low number still leaves room to walk away.

For HDB resale flats the valuation is HDB's, and the timing is fixed by the Option to Purchase:

  • The option period is 21 calendar days from the date the seller grants the OTP.
  • Submit a Request for Value on My Flat Dashboard by the next working day after the option date, with a scan of page 1 of the OTP, and pay HDB's S$120 fee, GST included. Your salesperson can submit it for you.
  • The outcome usually arrives within 10 working days, and you must wait for it before exercising the OTP.
  • It is valid for 3 months. Buyer and seller must both submit their parts of the resale application within that time, or a new request is needed.
  • Cash-only buyers, using no CPF and no loan, do not need one.

HDB's value becomes the basis for your CPF usage and the reference for your loan, whether the loan is from HDB or a bank, unless the bank says otherwise. Anything you agreed above it is cash over valuation, payable in cash. Because the valuation arrives before you exercise the OTP, a disappointing number still leaves you room to renegotiate or walk away, at the cost of the option fee.

Private Property: Bank Valuations

For condos and landed homes there is no single official value. Each bank instructs a valuer from its own panel, and the valuer looks at recent comparable sales in the development and nearby. Three things follow:

  • Get an indicative valuation before you commit. Banks will value a specific unit before you take an option, usually free. It is not binding, but it tells you whether the price is supportable.
  • Banks disagree. The same unit can be valued a few per cent apart by different banks. On a S$1.5 million condo, 3% is S$45,000 of valuation, and a S$33,750 difference in the loan at 75%.
  • New launches are different. Banks value an uncompleted unit off the developer's own price list, so valuation gaps are rarer at launch and more common on resale. See our new launch financing guide.

Valuation When You Refinance or Cash Out

Valuation matters again whenever you borrow against a property you already own. When you refinance, the new bank values the home to set its loan, and a lower valuation can mean a smaller loan or a slightly higher rate tier. When you take cash out, the amount you can release is a percentage of today's valuation, less what you owe and the CPF you used, so the valuation sets the ceiling directly. It is worth checking indicative valuations from several banks before you choose one.

If the Valuation Comes In Low

  1. Renegotiate. A written valuation is the strongest argument you have for a lower price, especially on resale.
  2. Ask another bank. For private property, a second or third indicative valuation can close the gap. A broker can request several at once.
  3. Fund the gap, knowingly. If you still want the property, make sure the extra cash is there before you exercise the option, not after.
  4. Walk away within the option period. On an HDB resale flat you see HDB's value before you exercise the OTP, so you can decline and lose only the option fee.

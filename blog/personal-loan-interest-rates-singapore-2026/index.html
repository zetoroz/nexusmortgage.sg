Personal Loan Interest Rates in Singapore: Bank by Bank, With the Real Cost

By Dan Ler, Mortgage Advisor

Short answer

As at 21 October 2026, the main banks advertised personal loans from 0.90% to 1.98% a year flat, which is an EIR from about 1.75% to 4.19%. Those are best-case rates. Your offer depends on your income and credit, and fees change the real cost: DBS's own example at 1.99% plus a 1% fee is an EIR of 5.47%. Always compare on the EIR including fees, and if you plan to buy or refinance a home soon, remember the instalment cuts your home loan by far more than it costs.

The spread between the cheapest and dearest is wide, and even a percentage point of EIR adds up over a three or five year loan. But the bigger gap is usually between the advertised rate and the one you are actually offered.

Why “From” Is Not Your Rate

Every rate in that table is a “from” rate: the lowest the bank offers, to its strongest applicants. Banks personalise the rate on your income, your credit report, your existing borrowing and often whether you already bank with them.

DBS shows the gap in its own worked example: a S$3,000 loan over 12 months at 1.99% plus a 1% processing fee is an EIR of 5.47%, against its headline 1.48% and EIR of 3.22%. A smaller loan, a shorter tenure and a fixed fee all push the effective rate up.

There are also hard limits on how much each bank can lend you, set by MAS on your income: 2 months' income if you earn S$20,000 to below S$30,000 a year, 4 months' from S$30,000 to below S$120,000. Our personal loan limits guide covers them in full.

What S$30,000 Really Costs

Compare on the total cost and the EIR, not the monthly instalment.

Personal loans charge a flat rate on the full original amount for the whole tenure, even though you are repaying every month, so the effective rate is roughly double. We explain why in our flat rate vs EIR guide. On S$30,000 over three years:

The instalments look close together, which is why borrowers tend to pick on the monthly figure. The total cost is where the difference shows: over six times as much between the cheapest and the dearest row.

The Fees That Change the Answer

  • Processing fees, usually a percentage of the loan taken off what you receive. You pay interest on money you never got, which is why a fee lifts the EIR.
  • Annual fees on some products, such as the S$199 on Standard Chartered CashOne or Trust's 0.88% first-year fee.
  • Early termination fees if you repay ahead of schedule, for example S$250 on a DBS personal loan. Check this if you might clear the loan early with a bonus or a property sale.
  • Late payment charges, plus the knock-on damage to your credit report.

A bank's EIR already includes its standard fees, which is why it is the number to compare. Cash rewards and promotions do not change the EIR; weigh them separately, and only if the loan was the right one anyway.

What It Does to Your Home Loan

This is the part most comparison tables leave out. A personal loan instalment counts against your 55% TDSR, and home loans are sized at a stress-test rate, so each dollar of instalment removes far more than a dollar of home loan. A S$500 monthly instalment removes about S$94,700 of home loan capacity at 4% over 25 years, assuming it is your only other debt.

How to Compare

  • Compare the EIR, not the flat rate, for the amount and tenure you actually want.
  • Check your own rate in each bank's app or with a quote; the advertised rate is the floor.
  • Read the fee schedule, especially the early termination fee.
  • Borrow only what you need, for the shortest tenure you can afford.
  • Think about your next home loan before you sign, not after.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which bank has the lowest personal loan interest rate in Singapore?

As at 21 October 2026, Standard Chartered CashOne at 0.90% a year flat (EIR from 1.75%), then UOB and Trust at 1.00%, HSBC at 1.30%, DBS at 1.48% and OCBC (card holders) at 1.98%. These are best-case rates; yours depends on your profile.

What is the difference between the interest rate and the EIR on a personal loan?

The flat rate is charged on the original loan for the whole tenure. The EIR is the true annual cost on the balance you owe, including fees, and is roughly double. Compare on the EIR.

Why is the rate I am offered higher than the advertised rate?

Advertised rates are for the strongest applicants. DBS's own example uses 1.99% plus a 1% fee, an EIR of 5.47%, against its headline 1.48%.

What fees should I look out for on a personal loan?

Processing fees, annual fees on some products, early termination fees (S$250 at DBS) and late payment charges. Standard Chartered CashOne has a S$199 annual fee.

How much can I borrow on a personal loan?

Up to 2 months' income per bank if you earn S$20,000 to below S$30,000 a year, or 4 months' from S$30,000 to below S$120,000, with no MAS cap from S$120,000. Unsecured interest-bearing debt across all banks and card issuers should stay under 12 times monthly income.

Will a personal loan affect my home loan?

Yes. A S$500 monthly instalment removes about S$94,700 of home loan capacity at 4% over 25 years, assuming it is your only other debt.
